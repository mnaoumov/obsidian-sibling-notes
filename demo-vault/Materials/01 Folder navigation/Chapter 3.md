The track comes down to the river at a weir, where the water drops a few feet over a concrete lip and churns white below it. The noise is loud after the quiet of the ridge, and a fine spray hangs in the air when the wind blows from the far side.

Below the weir the river widens into a pool. Children swim here in summer, and a rope hangs from a branch of the big willow on the near bank, frayed at the end. The water is clear enough to see the stones on the bottom, and small fish hold still in the current.

The path follows the pool round to a gate, and beyond the gate is the lane that leads back into the village. The first houses have gardens that run down to the water, with boats pulled up on the grass and tables set out under the trees.
