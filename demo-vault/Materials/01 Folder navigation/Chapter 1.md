# Chapter 1

The first chapter of a short walk along a river, long enough that the two navigator bars are apart: one sits above this paragraph, after the title, and the other waits at the end of the note. Step to the next item to reach [Chapter 2](<./Chapter 2.md>).

The path starts at the old stone bridge, where the water runs fast and shallow over a bed of grey pebbles. In the morning the light comes in low across the fields, and the far bank is still half in shadow. A heron stands in the reeds and does not move while you pass.

Past the bridge the path narrows between two hedges. Blackberries grow on both sides, too sour this early in the season, and the brambles catch at your sleeves if you walk too close. Every few hundred steps a gap in the hedge shows the river again, wider now and slower.

After a mile the hedges give way to an open meadow. The grass is long and wet, and the path is only a line where other walkers have pressed it flat. A wooden bench stands at the edge of the meadow, facing the water, with a small brass plate that has worn too smooth to read.

This is a good place to stop. Sit on the bench, and watch the current carry leaves and twigs past in a steady line. When you are ready, scroll down: the bottom bar is just below this paragraph, and its Next button goes on to the second chapter.
